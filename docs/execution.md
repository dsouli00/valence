# Tunisia Startup Execution Plan (Then KSA/Qatar Expansion)

You asked for real-time, actionable steps (not generic advice).  
This file saves the practical launch plan, starting with Tunisia and then expanding to GCC.

## 1) This week (48–72 hours): lock startup basics

- Define your offer in 1 sentence: “We help [target customer] achieve [result] with [product].”
- Pick 1 niche only (example: gyms, clinics, SMBs, etc.).
- Validate pain with 20 customer interviews (minimum).
- Build a waitlist + landing page (collect name/email/phone + pain point).
- KPI to continue: at least 30% of interviews say they would pay.

## 2) Week 1: legal launch checklist in Tunisia

- Choose legal form (usually SUARL or SARL for early stage).
- Reserve company name.
- Register company in RNE (Registre National des Entreprises).
- Get tax ID / patente, open company bank account.
- Register social security (CNSS) if hiring.
- If tech startup: apply for Startup Label (Startup Act Tunisia) once entity is ready.
- Open accounting setup with a local accountant immediately (monthly compliance).

## 3) Week 1–2: financial + business plan (investor-ready)

Prepare these 5 documents:

- 1-page executive summary.
- 12–18 month operating budget (fixed costs, salaries, CAC, runway).
- 3-year financial model (revenue assumptions, gross margin, burn, break-even).
- Go-to-market plan (acquisition channels + monthly targets).
- Risk plan (legal, cashflow, customer concentration).

## 4) Week 2: pitch deck that can raise money

Use this 10-slide structure:

- Problem
- Solution
- Product demo
- Market size (Tunisia first, MENA expansion)
- Business model
- Traction (users, pilots, LOIs)
- GTM
- Competition + moat
- Team
- Ask (how much, use of funds, milestones)

## 5) Month 1–2: traction before fundraising

- Run 2–3 paid pilots in Tunisia (not free if possible).
- Track weekly: leads, conversion, CAC, retention, MRR.
- Get 3 client testimonials/case studies.
- Target first repeatable sales channel (partner, outbound, community, ads).

## 6) Funding path (Tunisia first)

- Start with grants/incubators/accelerators + angel investors.
- Raise only after proving early traction (pilot revenue + retention).
- Keep data room ready: legal docs, cap table, financial model, contracts, KPI dashboard.

## 7) Expansion design from day 1 (so GCC entry is easier)

- Use English-first contracts and bilingual product (EN/AR).
- Build compliance-ready policies (privacy, terms, invoicing).
- Keep unit economics per market (CAC/LTV) in your dashboard.
- Identify what localizes by country: payments, regulation, procurement process.

## 8) GCC expansion roadmap (after Tunisia PMF)

Entry order:

- Step 1: Saudi (largest demand, strongest B2B budgets)
- Step 2: Qatar (smaller but high-value contracts)

Saudi (KSA) setup:

- Validate demand with 15–20 Saudi customer interviews first.
- Choose entry model: distributor/partner first, then entity.
- For direct operations/investment, check MISA licensing route + legal entity requirements.
- Localize: Arabic sales materials, local payment/invoicing/tax handling.

Qatar setup:

- Choose route: mainland entity or QFC structure based on your activity.
- Validate procurement path (private vs gov clients).
- Build local partner channel early.

## 9) Decision gates (don’t expand too early)

Expand only if Tunisia hits:

- Consistent monthly growth
- Proven retention
- Positive signal on payback period
- Repeatable sales process
